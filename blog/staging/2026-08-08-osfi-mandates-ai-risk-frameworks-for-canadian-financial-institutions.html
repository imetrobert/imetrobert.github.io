Executive Perspective

From Robert's Desk

The constant drumbeat of new model releases obscures a deeper, more important shift: the AI market is bifurcating. One path leads to ever-larger, multi-modal frontier models from the hyperscalers. The other, often overlooked, is a relentless drive for efficiency and cost reduction in smaller, specialized models. Many executives still chase the headline models, believing bigger is always better. They are missing the opportunity to deploy highly effective, cheaper models for 80% of their enterprise tasks.

Organizations often get stuck between pilot projects and enterprise-wide deployment. The technical challenges are usually solved quickly. The real friction comes from internal governance, data quality, and the inability of business units to agree on a common definition of success. Without clear ownership for data pipelines and outcome metrics, even the most promising AI initiatives will stall, regardless of model capability. We see this pattern repeat across industries.

My assessment is that the focus on "AI sovereignty" will intensify, especially in Canada. The discussions at VB Transform, particularly from Cohere, underscore this. It is not just about where the data sits. It is about control over the entire AI stack, from compute to models to governance. Canadian businesses must demand this level of control from their vendors, or risk becoming dependent on foreign infrastructure and policy. This is a business resilience issue, not just a technical one.

The risk executives are overlooking is the quiet erosion of public trust. Government consultations on AI transparency are a direct response to this. If companies deploy AI without clear communication about its use and limitations, they invite a regulatory backlash. Transparency is not just a compliance checkbox; it is foundational to sustained adoption and social license.
