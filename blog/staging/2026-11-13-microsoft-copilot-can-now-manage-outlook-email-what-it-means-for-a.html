Robert's Perspective

From Robert's Desk

The biggest shift I see this month is AI moving from a chat window to directly managing work inside core business applications. For years, the promise of AI was automation, but the reality was often "AI-assisted drafting." Now, we are seeing tools like Microsoft Copilot take over actual email triage, or Google Gemini letting you edit documents without leaving the AI. This is where the real time savings begin for a small team.

The common pattern I observe is that owners buy a tool, but the work itself does not change. The software sits there, used for a clever demo, but never integrated into the daily grind. The difference with this new wave is that the AI is embedded where the work already happens. It is not an extra step; it is a new capability in the tools you already pay for. This makes adoption much easier because it requires less behavioural change.

The catch owners are overlooking is the need for clear instructions. These AI systems are powerful, but they are not mind-readers. If you want Copilot to manage your inbox, you need to define "important" or "urgent" in a way the AI can understand. This means spending a little time upfront to structure your requests and refine your prompts. It is the same discipline required for any effective delegation, just applied to a digital assistant.

Small businesses have an advantage here. They do not have layers of management or committees to approve new workflows. An owner can experiment, adapt, and implement a new process in days, not months. The businesses that get value will be the ones willing to define the work for the AI, rather than waiting for the AI to magically figure it out.
