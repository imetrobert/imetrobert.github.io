What This Means for Canadian Business

The Government of Canada's July 23rd launch of an AI transparency consultation signals a clear direction. This move, detailed in Canada's National AI Strategy: AI for All, demands Canadian businesses prepare for more stringent AI oversight. Expect increased requirements for identifying AI-generated content and clarifying user interactions with AI systems.

Statistics Canada's July 30th report on generative AI use among workers highlights significant adoption in professional, scientific, and technical services. This adoption, reaching 35.9% of workers, contrasts with lower rates in sectors like accommodation and food services. Canadian firms must assess their own AI integration against industry benchmarks and competitor strategies.

The ongoing regulatory discussions, underscored by the July 23rd consultation, mean compliance deadlines are approaching. Canadian companies must proactively address data quality and governance issues, as identified in the RSM Canada survey, to avoid falling behind. Proving ROI remains a critical hurdle for scaling AI initiatives.

Strategic Actions for This Month

1
Audit AI-generated content policies: Review and update internal guidelines for identifying and labeling AI-generated content used in marketing, communications, and customer service, responding to the Government of Canada's July 23rd transparency consultation. Assign the Legal team and Marketing department to complete this by August 31.
2
Pilot generative AI for customer support: Test generative AI tools for specific customer service tasks, as seen with OpenAI's Presence product, to improve efficiency and response times. The Customer Service department should pilot this by September 15.
3
Assess AI compute needs: Evaluate current and future AI compute requirements in light of AMD's advancements and the AI Compute Access Fund expansion. The CTO should commission this assessment by August 15.
4
Enhance AI talent development: Continue or expand partnerships with institutions like the Vector Institute, following RBC's lead, to secure and develop AI talent. The CHRO should block time for this discussion with the Vector Institute by August 1.
5
Review AI data governance: Strengthen data governance frameworks to address data quality and security concerns, as highlighted by RSM Canada's findings, to enable successful AI scaling. The Data Governance Committee should review and propose updates by September 30.
