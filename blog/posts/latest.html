Executive Perspective

From Robert's Desk

The sheer volume of AI model updates this month is a clear signal. OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic are not just iterating; they are fundamentally changing what's possible with large language models. The push for larger context windows and multimodal capabilities means that the AI systems of just a year ago are now toys. What many Canadian executives are still missing is that this isn't about incremental improvements. It's a fundamental shift in the tools available for business problem-solving.

The risk I see is that most organizations will treat these advancements as procurement decisions, routing them through IT or procurement departments. This is a mistake. The real value of these new models lies in their ability to tackle complex, nuanced business problems that were previously intractable. Think about legal document review, sophisticated market analysis, or even personalized patient care pathways. The decision to adopt these tools needs to be driven by the business units that stand to gain the most, with Legal and Risk officers ensuring compliance, not by IT alone.

I expect the talent war for AI expertise to intensify dramatically. Canada has strong AI research hubs, but the global demand for skilled professionals is outpacing supply. Companies that can offer access to cutting-edge tools and compelling projects will win. Those that don't provide this environment will struggle to attract and retain the talent needed to drive AI adoption. My assessment is that Canadian businesses need to move beyond simply hiring AI talent and focus on creating an ecosystem where that talent can thrive and deliver tangible business outcomes. This means investing in compute, fostering a culture of experimentation, and aligning AI initiatives with clear business objectives.
